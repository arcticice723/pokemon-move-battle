## 1. Information Nexus may handle

Nexus currently has account and game features in development. Based on the current code, the service may handle:
- Account username and email address.
- A password hash used to verify sign-in; the password itself should not be stored.
- Profile picture data selected by the account holder.
- A signed-in session cookie.
- Temporary game information such as room codes, player names, moves, character guesses, answers, and chat messages.
- Basic technical information that may be recorded by the hosting provider, such as connection and request logs.

Friends, online status, game history, purchases, subscriptions, analytics, and advertising must not be described as active collection until those features and their actual data flows are implemented and verified.

## 2. Why information is used

Information may be used to create and secure accounts, show profiles, operate multiplayer rooms, prevent abuse, respond to support requests, and maintain the service. If optional paid features are introduced, the service may also need records of purchases and subscription status.

Nexus should not collect precise location, contact lists, or unrelated sensitive information for the initial game hub.

## 3. Who can see information

Other players may see the display name and game information needed to play a room. Email addresses, password hashes, session tokens, and private account details must never be sent to other players. Profile pictures and online status should follow the account holder's privacy settings.

Service providers may process information to host the website, database, or other enabled features. The final published policy must identify the actual providers and explain relevant processing.

## 4. Children and teen users

Nexus is intended for a general audience and should use age-appropriate safeguards. The operator must assess whether children's privacy laws apply before allowing under-13 users or knowingly collecting their personal information. Where applicable law requires parental notice or consent, the service must implement that process before collecting covered information. An age checkbox alone is not a substitute for legally required consent.

## 5. Storage and security

Nexus should use a persistent, access-controlled database for production accounts, a stable secret stored only in the hosting provider's secret settings, HTTPS, password hashing, restricted database access, and backups. No online service can promise perfect security.

The operator must verify actual hosting logs, backup behavior, vendors, and retention settings before publishing this section.

## 6. Retention and deletion

Users should be able to request or initiate account deletion. The service should delete or de-identify account data that it no longer needs, subject to lawful recordkeeping, security, dispute-resolution, and backup-retention needs. The final policy must state the actual retention periods and explain any exceptions.

## 7. Privacy choices and legal rights

Depending on the user's location and the laws that apply, users may have rights to access, correct, delete, obtain, or limit certain uses of personal information. The operator must provide a working way to submit privacy requests and respond within applicable deadlines.

## 8. Advertising and sale of data

The initial plan is not to sell personal information or use targeted advertising. If analytics, advertising, or other tracking tools are later added, this policy and the consent/opt-out experience must be reviewed and updated before those tools are enabled.
